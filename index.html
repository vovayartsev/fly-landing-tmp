The hard part is rarely one ticket.

When a journey crosses transport systems and separate bookings, the search itself becomes a project.

Days of research

The answer is scattered across airline websites, rail timetables, airport pages and travel forums, all in separate tabs.

Where it began

One family trip. Too many moving parts.

A family of four needed to travel from Valencia to Samara on the Volga for Christmas. There were no direct flights. Search engines returned either nothing or seven dates of essentially the same route.

The real choices were different corridors: via Istanbul, Yerevan or the Gulf; overland across a land border; by train for the final leg; or through a closed regional airport with a 50 km taxi detour. Comparing them took days of manual research.

Krugalia is being built to automate that work: find the possible paths first, then help understand which ones are actually practical.

Compare different corridors to the same destination — not just different dates for the same flight.

How it works

A conversation that turns into a checked plan.

Start with the trip in your head. Krugalia helps turn it into several routes you can compare.

1

Describe the trip naturally

Type or say where you're going, when, and who is travelling. No search form to fill out.

2

Clarify what matters

The assistant asks a few short questions and suggests likely answers you can simply confirm.

3

Compare genuinely different routes

See separate corridors — not the same itinerary repeated on different dates — with legs, times, group price estimates and trade-offs.

4

Check what can break a connection

Airport connection time, landside transfers, passport control, visa rules, terminal changes, night waits and ground transfers.

5

Know what is still uncertain

Every claim is marked checked, risk or not checked. Prices stay approximate until confirmed on the carrier's site.

What's new

Search the connections, not just the tickets.

Krugalia starts from how places connect, then reasons about the practical details of travelling between them.

A route graph, not a fare cache

Krugalia builds a connectivity graph of airports, stations and ground links from schedule data, then searches paths through it — including routes nobody has priced before.

Diversity by design

It deliberately returns different corridors and explains the trade-offs: price, time, risk and comfort — not 200 near-identical results.

Self-transfer awareness

Separate tickets, landside vs. airside transfers, passport control, visas for the actual passports, terminal changes and overnight waits are part of the search — not footnotes.

A conversation, not a form

Preferences such as “a night in a terminal is bad but not forbidden” or “three hours faster isn't worth €200” are understood from dialogue and change the ranking.

Grounded in sources

Rules and airport facts come from a curated knowledge base of official sources. When something cannot be verified, the assistant says so instead of guessing.

Honest by default

Clarity about what is known — and what isn't.

Krugalia separates verified facts from potential problems and unanswered questions. An estimated fare is never presented as a confirmed ticket price.

Built on travel data

A connected picture of the journey.

Today, Krugalia combines flight schedules and airport connectivity from AeroDataBox, rail timetables, approximate airline prices from public fare data, and a curated knowledge base of entry rules and airport facts.

It does not sell tickets or take bookings. Travellers buy directly from the relevant carrier.

Price confirmation, in your browser

A free Chrome extension is in progress. It will read live prices on airline and rail websites in your own browser tabs, so you can confirm fares without Krugalia scraping those sites.
